**Logline:** In a rigid 19th-century world, a brilliant mathematician, burdened by her father's scandalous legacy, defies societal expectations to forge an extraordinary partnership with an eccentric inventor, ultimately envisioning a future where machines can compose music and create art, becoming the world's first computer programmer.

**Three-Act Plot Outline:**

**Act 1: The Seed of Genius**
*   **Introduction to Ada's World:** We meet young Ada Lovelace, a child of immense intellect and curiosity, but also of conflicting legacies. Her mother, Lady Byron, attempts to steer her away from the "madness" of her poet father, Lord Byron, through rigorous mathematical education. Despite her mother's intentions, Ada's mind is drawn to both logic and imagination, a unique blend she later calls "poetical science."
*   **Societal Constraints and Early Mentors:** Ada struggles with frequent childhood illnesses and the restrictive expectations placed upon women of her era. Her intellectual hunger leads her to Mary Somerville, a pioneering female scientist, who recognizes Ada's exceptional talent and introduces her to the vibrant scientific salons of London.
*   **The Encounter with Babbage:** At one of these soirées, Ada, now eighteen, is introduced to the eccentric and visionary Charles Babbage and his fantastical, unbuilt Analytical Engine. While others see a complex calculating machine, Ada sees something more profound – a glimmer of a future where machines can manipulate symbols beyond mere numbers.

**Act 2: The Visionary Partnership**
*   **Deepening Collaboration:** Ada and Babbage's professional relationship blossoms into a deep intellectual friendship. Babbage is initially captivated by Ada's sharp mind and her ability to grasp the complexities of his machine. Ada takes on the task of translating Luigi Menabrea's article on the Analytical Engine, but her extensive notes far surpass a simple translation, becoming the heart of her groundbreaking work.
*   **The Birth of the Program:** Through her "Notes," Ada articulates her revolutionary concept: the Analytical Engine is not just a calculator, but a general-purpose machine capable of executing a sequence of operations – a program. She writes what is widely considered the first computer program, an algorithm for calculating Bernoulli numbers, demonstrating the machine's potential.
*   **Conflict and Foresight:** Ada's vision extends beyond Babbage's own. While he focuses on numerical computation, she foresees the machine's ability to process any form of information – music, art, text. This leads to intellectual friction, as Babbage struggles to fully grasp the philosophical implications of her ideas. Ada faces skepticism from the wider scientific community and the pressures of her personal life, including her health and her role as a wife and mother, which often pull her away from her work.

**Act 3: The Unseen Legacy**
*   **Unfulfilled Potential:** Despite Ada's brilliant insights and Babbage's tireless efforts, the Analytical Engine remains unbuilt due to lack of funding and technological limitations of the era. This leads to a period of frustration and disappointment for Ada, as her grand vision seems destined to remain on paper.
*   **Enduring Belief:** Ada, however, never loses faith in the potential of her ideas. Even as her health declines and financial struggles mount, she continues to champion the Analytical Engine and her "poetical science," believing that one day, her concepts will revolutionize the world. She understands that her work is not just about a machine, but about a new way of thinking about intelligence and creativity.
*   **A Future Remembered:** The film concludes with a poignant reflection on Ada's life and work. Though she may not live to see her vision realized, her notes are preserved, a testament to her extraordinary foresight. The final scenes could symbolically depict the dawn of the digital age, with a montage of modern computers and their capabilities, underscoring how Ada Lovelace's "poetical science" laid the foundational blueprint for the information age, cementing her legacy as the world's first computer programmer and a true visionary.
